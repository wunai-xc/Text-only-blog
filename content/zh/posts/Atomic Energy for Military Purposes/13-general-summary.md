### PRESENT OVERALL STATUS 当前总体状况

13.1. As the result of the labors of the Manhattan District organization in Washington and in Tennessee, of the scientific groups at Berkeley, Chicago, Columbia, Los Alamos, and else- where, of the industrial groups at Clinton, Hanford, and many other places, the end of June 1945 finds us expecting from day to day to hear of the explosion of the first atomic bomb devised by man. All the problems are believed to have been solved at least well enough to make a bomb practicable. A sustained neutron chain reaction resulting from nuclear fission has been demonstrated; the conditions necessary to cause such a reaction to occur explosively have been established and can be achieved; production plants of several different types are in operation, building up a stock pile of the explosive material. Although we do not know when the first explosion will occur nor how effective it will be, announcement of its occurrence will precede the publication of this report. Even if the first attempt is relatively ineffective, there is little doubt that later efforts will be highly effective; the devastation from a single bomb is expected to be comparable to that of a major air raid by usual methods.

由于曼哈顿工程区在华盛顿和田纳西的努力，伯克利、芝加哥、哥伦比亚、洛斯阿拉莫斯等地的科学团队的努力，克林顿、汉福德以及许多其他地方工业团队的努力，到1945年6月底，我们预计随时都会听到人类制造的第一颗原子弹爆炸的消息。据信所有问题都已解决，至少足以使原子弹成为可行。由核裂变产生的持续中子链式反应已经得到证实；使这种反应以爆炸方式发生所必需的条件已经确立并可以实现；几种不同类型的生产工厂正在运行，建立起这种爆炸材料的库存。虽然我们不知道第一次爆炸将在何时发生，也不知道其效果如何，但关于其发生的公告将先于本报告的发表。即使第一次尝试效果相对有限，也几乎毋庸置疑，后来的努力将非常有效；预计单颗炸弹的破坏力可与常规方法进行一次大规模空袭相当。

13.2. A weapon has been developed that is potentially destructive beyond the wildest nightmares of the imagination; a weapon so ideally suited to sudden unannounced attack that a country’s major cities might be destroyed overnight by an ostensibly friendly power. This weapon has been created not by the devilish inspiration of some warped genius but by the arduous labor of thousands of normal men and women working for the safety of their country. Many of the principles that have been used were well known to the international scientific world in 1940. To develop the necessary industrial processes from these principles has been costly in time, effort, and money, but the processes which we selected for serious effort have worked and several that we have not chosen could probably be made to work. We have an initial advantage in time because, so far as we know, other countries have not been able to carry out parallel developments during the war period. We also have a general advantage in scientific and particularly in industrial strength, but such an advantage can easily be thrown away.

已经研制出一种具有潜在破坏力的武器，其破坏力超出想象中最疯狂的噩梦；这种武器如此适合发动突然袭击而无需事先警告，以至一个表面上友好的国家可能在一夜之间摧毁另一个国家的主要城市。这种武器的创造，并非出自某个扭曲天才的恶魔般灵感，而是成千上万普通男女为国家安全而辛勤劳动的结果。其中使用的许多原理在1940年已为国际科学界所熟知。从这些原理开发出必要的工业流程，在时间、努力和金钱上都代价高昂，但我们选择认真努力的流程已经奏效，而我们未选择的若干流程很可能也能实现。我们在时间上拥有初始优势，因为据我们所知，其他国家在战争期间未能进行平行开发。我们在科学实力、特别是工业实力上也拥有总体优势，但这种优势很容易被丢弃。

13.3. Before the surrender of Germany there was always a chance that German scientists and engineers might be developing atomic bombs which would be sufficiently effective to alter the course of the war. There was therefore no choice but to work on them in this country. Initially many scientists could and did hope that some principle would emerge which would prove that atomic bombs were inherently impossible. This hope has faded gradually; fortunately in the same period the magnitude of the necessary industrial effort has been demonstrated so that the fear of German success weakened even before the end came. By the same token, most of us are certain that the Japanese cannot develop and use this weapon effectively.

在德国投降之前，始终存在一种可能：德国科学家和工程师可能正在研制原子弹，其效力足以改变战争进程。因此，我国别无选择，只能开展这方面的研制。起初，许多科学家能够并且确实希望会出现某种原理，证明原子弹在本质上不可能实现。这种希望逐渐消退；幸运的是，在同一时期，所需工业努力的规模已经显现，因此对德国成功的担忧在战争结束之前就已减弱。同样，我们大多数人确信，日本人无法有效地开发和使用这种武器。

### PROGNOSTICATION 预测

13.4. As to the future, one may guess that technical developments will take place along two lines. From the military point of view it is reasonably certain that there will be improvements both in the processes of producing fissionable material and in its use. It is conceivable that totally different methods may be dis- covered for converting matter into energy since it is to be remembered that the energy released in uranium fission corresponds to the utilization of only about one-tenth of one per cent of its mass. Should a scheme be devised for converting to energy even as much as a few percent of the matter of some common material, civilization would have the means to commit suicide at will.

至于未来，人们可以推测技术发展将沿两条路线进行。从军事角度看，可以合理确定，在生产裂变材料的流程及其使用方面都将有所改进。可以设想，可能会发现将物质转化为能量的完全不同的方法，因为必须记住，铀裂变释放的能量仅相当于其质量约千分之一的利用。假如设计出一种方案，能够把某种普通材料哪怕百分之几的物质转化为能量，文明就将拥有随意自杀的手段。

13.5. The possible uses of nuclear energy are not all destructive, and the second direction in which technical development can be expected is along the paths of peace. In the fall of 1944 General Groves appointed a committee to look into these possibilities as well as those of military significance. This committee (Dr. R. C. Tolman, chairman; Rear Admiral E. W. Mills (USN) with Captain T. A. Solberg (USN) as deputy, Dr. W. K. Lewis, and Dr. H. D. Smyth) received a multitude of suggestions from men on the various projects, principally along the lines of the use of nuclear energy for power and the use of radioactive by-products for scientific, medical, and industrial purposes. While there was general agreement that a great industry might eventually arise, comparable, perhaps, with the electronics industry, there was disagreement as to how rapidly such an industry would grow; the consensus was that the growth would be slow over a period of many years. At least there is no immediate prospect of running cars with nuclear power or lighting houses with radioactive lamps although there is a good probability that nuclear power for special purposes could be developed within ten years and that plentiful supplies of radioactive materials can have a profound effect on scientific research and perhaps on the treatment of certain diseases in a similar period.

核能的可能用途并非全是破坏性的，可以预期的技术发展的第二个方向是和平之路。1944年秋，格罗夫斯将军任命了一个委员会来研究这些可能性以及具有军事意义的可能性。该委员会（R. C.托尔曼博士任主席；海军少将 E. W. 米尔斯（E. W. Mills），以海军上校 T. A. 索尔伯格（T. A. Solberg）为副手，W. K.刘易斯博士和H. D.史密斯博士）收到了来自各项目人员的众多建议，主要涉及利用核能发电以及利用放射性副产物服务于科学、医学和工业目的。虽然人们普遍认为最终可能兴起一个庞大的工业，或许可与电子工业相比，但对于这种工业将以多快的速度成长存在分歧；多数意见认为，在许多年的时间里增长将是缓慢的。至少目前还没有用核动力驱动汽车或用放射性灯照亮住宅的近期前景，尽管很有可能在十年内开发出特殊用途的核动力，并且同样时期内充足的放射性材料供应可能对科学研究、或许还对某些疾病的治疗产生深远影响。

### PLANNING FOR THE FUTURE 未来的规划

13.6. During the war the effort has been to achieve the maxi- mum military results. It has been apparent for some time that some sort of government control and support in the field of nuclear energy must continue after the war. Many of the men associated with the project have recognized this fact and have come forward with various proposals, some of which were considered by the Tolman Committee, although it was only a temporary advisory committee reporting to General Groves. An interim committee at a high level is now engaged in formulating plans for a continuing organization. This committee is also discussing matters of general policy about which many of the more thoughtful men on the project have been deeply concerned since the work was begun and especially since success became more and more probable.

战争期间的努力一直是为了取得最大的军事成果。一段时间以来已经很明显，战后在核能领域必须继续有某种形式的政府控制和支持。与项目有关的许多人已经认识到这一事实，并提出了各种建议，其中一些由托尔曼委员会审议过，尽管该委员会只是一个向格罗夫斯将军报告的临时咨询委员会。目前一个高级别的临时委员会正在制定常设组织的计划。该委员会还在讨论一般政策问题，自工作开始以来、特别是自成功变得越来越可能以来，项目上许多较有思想的人一直深切关注这些问题。

### THE QUESTIONS BEFORE THE PEOPLE 摆在人民面前的问题

13.7. We find ourselves with an explosive which is far from completely perfected. Yet the future possibilities of such explosives are appalling, and their effects on future wars and international affairs are of fundamental importance. Here is a new tool for mankind, a tool of unimaginable destructive power. Its development raises many questions that must be answered in the near future.

我们发现自己拥有一种远未完全完善的爆炸物。然而，这类爆炸物的未来可能性令人震惊，它们对未来战争和国际事务的影响具有根本重要性。这是人类的一种新工具，一种具有难以想象的破坏力的工具。它的发展提出了许多必须在不久的将来加以回答的问题。

13.8. Because of the restrictions of military security there has been no chance for the Congress or the people to debate such questions. They have been seriously considered by all concerned and vigorously debated among the scientists, and the conclusions reached have been passed along to the highest authorities. These questions are not technical questions; they are political and social questions, and the answers given to them may affect all mankind for generations. In thinking about them the men on the project have been thinking as citizens of the United States vitally interested in the welfare of the human race. It has been their duty and that of the responsible high government officials who were informed to look beyond the limits of the present war and its weapons to the ultimate implications of these discoveries. This was a heavy responsibility. In a free country like ours, such questions should be debated by the people and decisions must be made by the people through their representatives. This is one reason for the release of this report. It is a semi-technical report which it is hoped men of science in this country can use to help their fellow citizens in reaching wise decisions. The people of the country must be informed if they are to discharge their responsibilities wisely.

由于军事安全的限制，国会或人民一直没有机会辩论这些问题。所有有关方面都对这些问题进行了认真考虑，科学家之间进行了激烈辩论，所得结论已转呈最高当局。这些问题不是技术问题；它们是政治和社会问题，对这些问题作出的回答可能影响全人类许多代。在思考这些问题时，项目上的人们一直以美国公民的身份思考，深切关心人类的福祉。他们以及知情的政府高级官员有责任超越当前战争及其武器的范围，看到这些发现的最终含义。这是一项沉重的责任。在我们这样的自由国家，这类问题应当由人民来辩论，决定必须由人民通过其代表作出。这是发表本报告的一个原因。这是一份半技术性报告，希望本国的科学界人士能够用它帮助同胞作出明智的决定。本国人民必须知情，才能明智地履行自己的责任。
