## 1 A brief introduction to antibacterial drugs

Antibacterial drugs are briefly called **antibiotics**; the official academic wording: *a class of secondary metabolites produced by microorganisms (including bacteria, fungi, and actinomycetes) or higher plants and animals during their life processes that have anti-pathogen or other activity — chemical substances that can interfere with the growth and development of other living cells.*

The definition of **antibiotic**: a class of chemical substances produced by certain microorganisms during their life processes that inhibit or kill certain other pathogenic microorganisms.

From the moment humanity discovered antibiotics, to mass-producing all kinds of antibiotics, the medical field today cannot do without them.

## 2 The history of antibiotics

### 2.1 · Sulfonamides · Prontosil

**Gerhard Johannes Paul Domagk (1895–1964)** discovered in 1932 that the red dye *Prontosil* could effectively suppress *streptococcal* infection in mice, and later successfully treated the streptococcal infection of his daughter.

Chemical structure of **Prontosil**:

```smiles title="Prontosil"
Nc1ccc(N=Nc2ccc(S(=O)(=O)N)cc2)c(N)c1
```

Chemical structure of **sulfanilamide**:

```smiles title="sulfanilamide"
"C1=CC(=CC=C1N)S(=O)(=O)N"
```

### 2.2 · Penicillin

In the late 1920s (1920–1929), **Alexander Fleming (1881–1955)** discovered *penicillin*.

> The story of Fleming discovering penicillin is presumably well known in China too.

Then during World War II (1941), **Fleming**, together with two other scientists **Howard Walter Florey (1898–1968)** and **Ernst Boris Chain (1906–1979)**, isolated high-purity crystalline *penicillin*.
